How to Use the Time Zone Planner

  1. The board opens with four default cities: Beijing, London, Zurich and New York.
  2. Drag the vertical line across the timeline — or click anywhere on it — to move through the day.
  3. Each city's local time and date update live as you drag, so you can see exactly what time it is for every participant.
  4. Colored bands mark business hours (green), early/late hours (amber) and off hours (dark) for each city, making it easy to spot a slot that works for everyone.
  5. Use "Add city" to bring in more locations, or the × button on a city to remove it.
  6. Click "Now" to jump back to the current time at any point.
  7. Use the date picker to check any past or future date — handy for confirming how each city's time shifts around daylight saving changes.

A Practical Guide to Scheduling Across Time Zones

A worked example: Beijing, Zurich and New York

Take the three cities most often paired in international teams. Beijing is UTC+8 all year. Zurich is UTC+1 in winter and UTC+2 in summer, and New York is UTC−5 in winter and UTC−4 in summer. Zurich and New York are always six hours apart, but Beijing is 12 or 13 hours ahead of New York, so 09:00–18:00 in New York is 21:00–06:00 (summer) or 22:00–07:00 (winter) in Beijing: those two business days never overlap. Zurich and New York share three working hours (15:00–18:00 in Zurich, 09:00–12:00 in New York); Zurich and Beijing share two hours in winter and three in summer.

No slot works for all three, so someone has to give something up. The least costly option is usually around 15:00 in Zurich: that is 09:00 in New York and 21:00 (summer) or 22:00 (winter) in Beijing. Putting the three timelines side by side is meant to find the slot where each person loses the least, rather than the one that happens to suit the organiser.

The daylight-saving gap that breaks recurring meetings

The United States moves to summer time on the second Sunday of March and back on the first Sunday of November. The EU changes on the last Sunday of March and the last Sunday of October. For roughly two to three weeks in spring and one to two weeks in autumn the two sides follow different rules, and the Zurich–New York gap shrinks from six hours to five. A recurring meeting fixed at 15:00 Zurich time then lands at 10:00 in New York instead of 09:00. China, India and Japan do not use daylight saving at all, and in the southern hemisphere it runs the other way round: Sydney is on summer time from October to April. The reliable habit is to check the date of the actual meeting, not today's date: set the date picker to that day and read off the offsets.

Time zones that break the pattern

Not every offset is a whole number of hours. India is UTC+5:30 all year and Nepal is UTC+5:45, so a 09:00 call in Zurich in summer (UTC+2) is 12:30 in India and 12:45 in Nepal. China uses a single official time, UTC+8, across the whole country, even though it is wide enough to span about five geographic zones. Assuming every zone sits a whole number of hours from UTC is the quickest way to arrive 30 minutes early or late.

Sharing the inconvenient slots fairly

When no time suits everyone, most of the resentment comes from the same office always being the one that stays late. Rotating the slot between regions, keeping recurring meetings short, recording them for people who cannot attend live, and quoting the time in UTC alongside local times all reduce the friction. Written invitations should always name the time zone, never just "evening".

UTC and GMT are not the same thing

UTC is the global time standard that every offset is measured from, and it never changes with the seasons. GMT is the time zone based on mean solar time at Greenwich and is used in the United Kingdom in winter. In summer the UK switches to British Summer Time (UTC+1), so London matches UTC only in winter. That is why the planner shows a UTC reference ruler: it is the one fixed line every other city can be compared against.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does the tool handle daylight saving time?

Each city's time is computed from its official IANA time zone, so daylight saving transitions are applied automatically and correctly for the current date.

Can I add cities other than the four defaults?

Yes. Use the "Add city" control to add any city from the list, and remove any row with its × button.

Does the tool save my selection?

Your chosen cities and language are saved in your browser's local storage, so they're remembered the next time you visit on the same device.

Does this tool send meeting invites?

No. It's a visual reference for finding a good time — copy the agreed time into your calendar or scheduling tool of choice.

Why do some cities show a different date than others?

Because time zones cross the international date line and UTC offsets differ, the same instant can fall on "today" in one city and "tomorrow" or "yesterday" in another. The date shown under each city's time reflects that.

Is this tool free to use?

Yes, the planner is free with no account required.
